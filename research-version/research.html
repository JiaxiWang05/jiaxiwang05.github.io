In submission ICLR 2027

EndoWAM: A Grounded World-Action Model for Generalizable Endoscopic Navigation

Jinsong Lin, Zikang Pan, Wanhao Liu, Chi Kit Ng, Liangjing Shao, Zihang Yu, Ziyu Wang, Yin Wang, Jiaxi Wang, Jeremy Yuen-Chun Teoh, Zhiyong Xiong, Huxin Gao, Hongliang Ren

Learned endoscopy policies usually act on the current frame alone, which makes them fragile under tissue deformation, occlusion and fast viewpoint change. EndoWAM is the first world-action model for generalisable robotic endoscopic navigation. It introduces future grounding: a lightweight diffusion transformer predicts task-relevant target regions in future frames from the intermediate denoising features of a video world model, and a discrete action expert shares that predictive representation, so control runs in real time from a single denoising pass. The paper also introduces EndoMotion, a robotic endoscopy dataset spanning ureteroscopy, oesophagoscopy and ERCP. EndoWAM outperforms all baselines and alternative grounding strategies and generalises zero-shot to unseen viewpoints, environments and targets.

Interactive Open Day robot

Supervised by Dr Amir Atapour-Abarghouei and Dr Chris Chen

A Unitree G1 humanoid that starts conversations at the Durham University open day. It notices visitors, decides when to speak, invites them over by naming something they wear, and chats with them, using what its camera sees.

  • Separates when to speak from what to say: on-robot person tracking (YOLO26n-pose with BoT-SORT) reads distance, facing, speed and groups, and a realtime speech model with vision (OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.1) writes the opening line and holds the conversation.
  • Grounded in a literature review of small vision–language models, realtime speech models, streaming video models that decide when to respond, and studies of how robots should start interactions in public spaces.
  • The program runs as one process on the robot's Jetson Orin NX and reaches the speech model online through a pocket 4G/5G router. It never moves the robot, and it refers only to clothing and objects, never to appearance.
  • Version 0.7 (November 2026): 170 tests pass on a laptop; not yet run on the robot.

Keywords human–robot interaction, proactive dialogue, vision–language models, realtime speech, humanoid robots

BEng Research and Development Project, 2026–27

Small data, large models: transfer learning for medical image classification

Supervised by Dr Raheleh Kafieh

Clinical datasets are small. This project measures how well a retinal foundation model detects glaucoma from colour fundus photographs when only a few hundred labelled images exist, and whether the result holds on new cameras and datasets. RETFound-Green, a compute-efficient retinal foundation model, turns each image into 384 features; simple classifiers are trained on these frozen features first, and partial fine-tuning of the encoder follows.

  • Data. Chákṣu: 1,345 images from three cameras, each graded by five experts (glaucoma by majority); PAPILA for external validation. Splits are grouped by eye or patient, after a check found that RETFound's released PAPILA split puts the two eyes of 121 of 244 patients on different sides.
  • Method. Logistic regression, SVM, k-nearest neighbours and an MLP, tuned with Optuna by 5-fold cross-validation on 1,141 images; a rule fixed in advance picks the simplest model tied with the best, and the held-out test set is used once.
  • Results so far. Logistic regression on frozen features: test AUROC 0.872 (95% CI 0.810–0.923) on 204 held-out images; at a threshold set for 90% sensitivity it found all 28 glaucoma images, at a specificity of 0.51. Regularising the MLP reduced its overfitting but did not raise AUROC, which points to the frozen representation or the labels as the limit.
  • Next. Partial fine-tuning of the encoder; transfer to PAPILA and to a camera left out of training, including whether the decision threshold transfers; and how expert agreement and training-set size limit performance.

Outputs: code, data splits and results are public on GitHub; a fine-tuning comparison, an external-validation table and a data-efficiency figure follow.
