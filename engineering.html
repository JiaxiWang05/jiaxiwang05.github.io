Engineering

Hardware projects from Durham University: an autonomous forklift, a wireless-powered artificial heart, a repeatable autonomous vehicle, and training in high-performance computing.

AGV

2024–2025. Group 19-AIBM4. Supervisors: Dr Aissa Ikhlef, Mr Bill Maxwell

Automated Material Mover

Co-designed and prototyped an automated guided vehicle that moves materials around a factory without a driver, and tested whether it is technically and financially feasible.

  • Mechanism. Three wheels with differential steering around a central axle, chosen for manoeuvrability in factory aisles.
  • Navigation. Tape-guided sensing for precise, repeatable paths.
  • Validation. Structural calculations, cost evaluation against manual forklifts, and a risk assessment, with the design checked against ISO 3691-4 for driverless industrial trucks.
  • Sustainability. Recyclable steel body and forks, and airless tyres for durability.

Feasibility report (PDF, 15 pages)

Heart

2024–2025. Supervisor: Prof. Ashraf W. Khir

Durham Total Artificial Heart

Wireless power and control electronics for an artificial heart: an interdisciplinary medical-engineering project that turned clinical needs for cardiovascular support into implementable engineering requirements.

  • Defined system requirements and partitioned the wireless powering solution into subsystems.
  • Coordinated development of high-efficiency power-transfer circuits, custom motors designed in ANSYS, and the control electronics.

Tools: LTspice, KiCad, ANSYS

Repeat

2023–2024

IMechE Repeatable Vehicle Challenge

Engineered, fabricated, calibrated and repeatedly tested an autonomous vehicle that travels a set distance, interacts with a target and returns to a repeatable stopping position, with no external control.
